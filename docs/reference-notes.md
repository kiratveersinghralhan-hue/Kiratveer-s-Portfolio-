# Reference study — 15 September 2026

Read-only browser study of the five user-supplied references. Home states were visually inspected in a hidden Codex in-app browser, including entry gates where present. No source artwork, copy, branding, or exact composition should be reused.

## Observed references

### [Remy Shoots](https://www.remyshoots.co.za/)

- A stark black/red entry presents a large wordmark and real loading progress, then lets visitors enter without audio.
- The gallery becomes the viewport: tall photographic strips, one dominant colour image, surrounding desaturated images, very small editorial metadata.
- Slider / grid / list modes and stills / motion filters expose different ways to browse the same work. The frame is stable while the imagery changes.
- Transferable principle: let actual work carry almost all visual weight; keep interface chrome precise. Do not copy its red palette, ruler motif, photographic arrangement, or sound defaults.

### [Studio Loop](https://www.studioloop.com.br/)

- The opening is a materially rich studio world: a physical chair, table, clothing, printed pieces and oversized branded form fill the stage.
- Deep brown, orange, ivory and pale lilac create a distinctive identity without relying on generic dark/blue technology conventions.
- Scrolling moves into giant numbered capability rows, with a selective serif/sans contrast and subtle horizontal rules.
- Transferable principle: 3D becomes believable when the objects share a coherent practice and material language. A Kirat book can connect real screenshots, print design and product thinking in this way.

### [Ben Lin / Ben X Run](https://www.benxrun.com/)

- Home is a full atmospheric skyline at twilight with quiet persistent chapter navigation.
- Chapters are given explicit names and ordinal numbers, with real URLs. Entering chapter 1 transitions through darkness to a full-scene illustration, typographic chapter title and a scroll prompt.
- Its scene supports a specific narrative, followed by actual readable essay content; the atmosphere is not the whole message.
- Transferable principle: create a meaningful story progression and let the visitor choose chapters. Do not reuse its skyline, story, illustration or chapter names.

### [Jamie McKaye](https://jamiemckaye.com/)

- The opening combines a large concise thesis, a clear professional label, direct buying actions and a tilted live-site representation.
- People / Search / Agents controls show different views of the same underlying site. Further down, a capability index and visible technical representations substantiate the positioning.
- Thin rules, strong type and tiny factual captions create confidence; the work itself supplies the proof.
- Transferable principle: every interaction should demonstrate the claimed capability. For Kirat this means a real project book and truthful product case studies, with equally clear contact routes.

### [CRÈCHE / the tank](https://www.crechetank.com/)

- A water-level loading scene resolves to an explicit Enter control.
- Main world is a pastel underwater/tank environment with translucent depth, small creature characters and floating conversational fragments.
- Scroll changes what is present in the world rather than simply revealing a vertical stack. Small navigation and persistent context sit outside the main scene.
- Sound was on after entry and was switched off during inspection. Kirat must retain the master requirement of sound off by default.
- Transferable principle: one complete spatial metaphor is more memorable than many unrelated effects. Do not borrow the tank, creatures, exact typography, colour world or dialogue.

## Original direction for Kirat: THE WORK, BOUND.

A digitally published, physical-feeling portfolio edition. Charcoal gallery space, warm ivory paper, deep cobalt ink and extremely restrained champagne edge details. Large clean sans-serif statements paired with editorial italic serif phrases. Kirat's real portrait is treated as a full-height editorial photograph, not a headshot card.

The story moves from **the maker** to **the volume** to **the worlds inside it** to **the collaboration**.

1. Opening: a short monogram-to-title reveal. State identity, offer and two clear actions immediately. A real portrait provides human specificity.
2. Manifesto: a single oversized statement whose emphasis changes as the reader moves into the book studio.
3. Signature book: a landscape/near-square clothbound art book resting in a dark studio. Real screenshot spreads, restrained page curl, paper edge depth, a deliberate hinge and warm grazing light. Camera moves closer before the cover opens. Turn pages to progress through real work. Native project links and chapter buttons accompany it.
4. Worlds: each featured project's spread resolves into an expansive interface presentation, with its own restrained colour treatment. QORTRA leads as an actively developed software product, and its public story separates built work from intent. Actual supplied UI is the exhibit.
5. Motion: a framed projection/film contact sheet with genuine video work. One clip selected at a time; audio stays off until requested.
6. Practice: typographic capability and process indices provide relief after the image-heavy narrative. Prices are a calm editorial list, not promotional cards.
7. Close: a second carefully selected real portrait and an oversized invitation resolve to prominent WhatsApp, email and a structured enquiry form.

## Implementation guardrails

- The physical book is the only demanding spatial scene. Avoid decorative spheres, abstract 3D filler and unrelated chrome objects.
- Native document order and anchor navigation remain the backbone. Scrolling changes camera, cover angle, page angle, crop and scene emphasis only where it helps comprehension.
- Mobile receives a designed magazine sequence with visible project titles, layered page planes, native scrolling and explicit tap actions. It should not inherit desktop scroll distances.
- Alternate warm paper and dark studio scenes to create rhythm, with cobalt reserved for AI/product emphasis and selected controls.
- Persist clear ways to contact Kirat and jump to work. An immersive experience still needs a short route to purchasing intent.

## Access limits

All five home experiences were successfully inspected visually. Remy entry and gallery, Loop opening and capability transition, Ben home plus chapter 1, Jamie hero/content structure, and Creche entry plus main spatial scene were viewed. This was an art-direction study, not an exhaustive interaction, mobile or accessibility audit of the references. The browser viewport was approximately 998×720, and some large desktop reference navigation extended beyond that width; do not repeat this issue in Kirat's implementation.
